# LORE Crypto History — 250-event timeline

Selection v1 · 26 September 2026 · **100 card moments + 150 supporting events.**

Coverage: foundations in 1976 through 26 September 2026. This is the editorial selection for review, not a claim that every date, long-form story or source has received final publication approval. The current year is marked provisional in historical perspective.

Card numbers link to the separate 100-moment review order; they are not final printed collector numbers. Full source provenance, dates, notes, review flags and card links are in `selection-v1.json`. Research not selected here remains in the year archive.

## 1976

**001. 1976-11 — Diffie and Hellman publish foundational public-key cryptography work**  
*Supporting event* — Diffie and Hellman published New Directions in Cryptography, introducing the public-key cryptography framework and digital-signature concept to the open literature.

[Source 1](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/24.pdf)

## 1982

**002. 1982 — David Chaum proposes cryptographic digital cash**  
*Card moment 001* — Chaum presented Blind Signatures for Untraceable Payments at CRYPTO '82, a direct foundation for privacy-preserving electronic cash.

[Source 1](https://chaum.com/publications/) · [Source 2](https://chaum.com/ecash/)

## 1988

**003. 1988 — The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto circulates**  
*Supporting event* — Timothy May stated that The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto dated to mid-1988 and was distributed at Crypto '88; he posted it electronically to the Cypherpunks list in November 1992.

[Source 1](https://www.activism.net/cypherpunk/crypto-anarchy.html)

## 1989

**004. 1989 — DigiCash is founded**  
*Supporting event* — DigiCash was founded by David Chaum to commercialize cryptographic electronic-cash technology.

[Source 1](https://chaum.com/ecash/) · [Source 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiCash)

## 1992

**005. 1992-09-21 — Cypherpunks mailing list emerges**  
*Supporting event* — Eric Hughes sent the welcome message establishing the Cypherpunks mailing list infrastructure on toad.com.

[Source 1](https://mailing-list-archive.cryptoanarchy.wiki/archive/1992/09/fdf9c19e77ec3f1a9bbc6bc19266d565b89d19dbd0ad369f5a2e800af3fc9558/)

## 1993

**006. 1993-03-09 — A Cypherpunk's Manifesto**  
*Card moment 002* — Eric Hughes published A Cypherpunk's Manifesto to the Cypherpunks mailing list.

[Source 1](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/cypherpunk-manifesto/)

## 1994

**007. 1994-05-27 — DigiCash conducts early electronic-cash trials with banks**  
*Supporting event* — Chaum's eCash history records DigiCash's first electronic cash payment over computer networks on 27 May 1994; broader CyberBucks trials followed in 1994.

[Source 1](https://chaum.com/ecash/)

## 1996

**008. 1996 — e-gold launches as an early digital value system**  
*Supporting event* — e-gold began offering an internet-based digital currency backed by precious metals in 1996.

[Source 1](https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/ceos/pressreleases/downloads/DC%20egold%20indictment.pdf)

## 1997

**009. 1997-05 — Adam Back creates Hashcash**  
*Card moment 003* — Adam Back's later Hashcash paper states that Hashcash was originally proposed in May 1997 as an anti-spam/proof-of-work mechanism.

[Source 1](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/hashcash/)

## 1998

**010. 1998-11 — Wei Dai publishes b-money**  
*Supporting event* — Wei Dai published b-money in November 1998, describing protocols for money and contracts among untraceable entities.

[Source 1](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/b-money/)

**011. 1998 — DigiCash files for bankruptcy**  
*Supporting event* — DigiCash entered bankruptcy in 1998 after failing to achieve broad commercial adoption.

[Source 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiCash)

## 2004

**012. 2004-08-15 — Hal Finney creates reusable proof-of-work**  
*Card moment 004* — Hal Finney announced RPOW, a server that exchanged Hashcash proofs for transferable reusable proof-of-work tokens.

[Source 1](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/rpow/)

## 2005

**013. 2005-12-29 — Nick Szabo describes Bit Gold**  
*Supporting event* — The surviving primary Bit Gold publication is Nick Szabo's 29 December 2005 post; it describes scarce digital objects created through proof of work and timestamping.

[Source 1](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold/)

## 2008

**014. 2008-08-18 — bitcoin.org domain is registered**  
*Supporting event* — The bitcoin.org domain was registered on 18 August 2008, more than two months before the whitepaper announcement.

[Source 1](https://bitcoin.org/en/posts/ten-year-anniversary)

**015. 2008-10-31 — Satoshi Nakamoto publishes the Bitcoin whitepaper**  
*Card moment 005* — Satoshi Nakamoto posted Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System to the Cryptography Mailing List on 31 October 2008.

[Source 1](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf) · [Source 2](https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/emails/cryptography/threads/1/)

## 2009

**016. 2009-01-03T18:15:05Z — Bitcoin Genesis Block is mined**  
*Card moment 006* — Bitcoin's genesis block was timestamped 3 January 2009 and embedded the Times headline about a possible second bank bailout.

[Source 1](https://chainquery.com/stories/genesis-block)

**017. 2009-01-09 — Bitcoin v0.1 software is publicly released**  
*Supporting event* — Satoshi announced Bitcoin v0.1 and made the software and source code publicly available through SourceForge.

[Source 1](https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/emails/cryptography/threads/2/)

**018. 2009-01-11T03:33:00Z — Hal Finney posts 'Running bitcoin'**  
*Supporting event* — Hal Finney publicly posted the two-word message 'Running bitcoin' while operating the new software.

[Source 1](https://bitcoinannotated.com/entries/running-bitcoin/)

**019. 2009-01-12T03:30:25Z — Satoshi sends Hal Finney 10 BTC**  
*Card moment 007* — Bitcoin block 170 contains the first known person-to-person Bitcoin transaction: 10 BTC sent from Satoshi to Hal Finney.

[Source 1](https://mempool.space/block/00000000d1145790a8694403d4063f323d499e655c83426834d4ce2f8dd4a2ee)

**020. 2009-10-05 — New Liberty Standard publishes an early Bitcoin-dollar exchange rate**  
*Supporting event* — New Liberty Standard published a rate of $1 = 1,309.03 BTC based on an estimate of the electricity cost of mining.

[Source 1](https://bitcoin-institute.pages.dev/participants/newlibertystandard/)

**021. 2009-10-12 — Martti Malmi completes an early documented BTC-for-fiat trade**  
*Supporting event* — New Liberty Standard bought 5,050 BTC from Martti Malmi for $5.02 via PayPal.

[Source 1](https://bitcoin-institute.pages.dev/participants/newlibertystandard/)

**022. 2009-11-22T18:04:28Z — Satoshi opens the new Bitcoin forum**  
*Supporting event* — Satoshi posted 'Welcome to the new Bitcoin forum!' on 22 November 2009, pointing users to the older SourceForge forum and beginning the forum archive later known as BitcoinTalk.

[Source 1](https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/posts/bitcointalk/threads/2/)

## 2010

**023. 2010-03-17 — BitcoinMarket.com launches**  
*Supporting event* — BitcoinMarket.com began operating as an early order-book Bitcoin/USD market.

[Source 1](https://bitcoinmarket.net/magazine/bitcoinmarket-first-bitcoin-exchange-history-en.html)

**024. 2010-05-10 — Laszlo Hanyecz demonstrates GPU Bitcoin mining**  
*Supporting event* — Laszlo Hanyecz posted a working OpenCL GPU miner and reported materially higher hashing performance than CPU mining.

[Source 1](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=133.0)

**025. 2010-05-22 — Bitcoin Pizza Day**  
*Card moment 008* — Laszlo Hanyecz reported successfully trading 10,000 BTC for pizza, thanking 'jercos.'

[Source 1](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=137.0)

**026. 2010-06-11 — Gavin Andresen launches the Bitcoin Faucet**  
*Card moment 009* — Gavin Andresen launched a faucet that gave each visitor 5 BTC, initially funding it with 1,100 BTC.

[Source 1](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=206)

**027. 2010-07-18 — Mt. Gox begins operating as a Bitcoin exchange**  
*Supporting event* — Jed McCaleb repurposed the mtgox.com domain and launched a Bitcoin exchange.

[Source 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Gox)

**028. 2010-08-15 — Bitcoin value overflow incident and emergency fix**  
*Card moment 010* — A value-overflow flaw was exploited in block 74,638 to create roughly 184.47 billion invalid BTC; developers rapidly patched the software and the valid chain rejected the bad history.

[Source 1](https://bitcoin-institute.pages.dev/entries/aftermath/2010-08-15-bitcoin-v0310-overflow-bug-fix/)

**029. 2010-11-27 — Slush Pool begins pooled Bitcoin mining**  
*Card moment 011* — Marek 'Slush' Palatinus announced a cooperative mining pool, widely recognized as the first public Bitcoin mining pool.

[Source 1](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1976)

## 2011

**030. 2011-01 (approximately) — Silk Road launches**  
*Supporting event* — US prosecutors later alleged that Ross Ulbricht created Silk Road in approximately January 2011 and operated it as an anonymous online marketplace using Bitcoin.

[Source 1](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/manhattan-us-attorney-announces-indictment-ross-ulbricht-creator-and-owner-silk-road)

**031. 2011-04-18 — Namecoin launches**  
*Supporting event* — Namecoin was released on 18 April 2011 as an early Bitcoin-derived blockchain focused on decentralized naming.

[Source 1](https://www.namecoin.org/2021/04/18/namecoin-released-10-years-ago-today.html)

**032. 2011-04-23 — Satoshi says he has moved on to other things**  
*Supporting event* — In his final known email to Mike Hearn, Satoshi wrote that he had 'moved on to other things' and that Bitcoin was in good hands with Gavin and everyone.

[Source 1](https://bitcoin-institute.pages.dev/entries/correspondence/mike-hearn/holding-coins/2011-04-23-satoshi-to-hearn-moved-on/)

**033. 2011-06-14 — WikiLeaks begins accepting Bitcoin donations**  
*Card moment 012* — WikiLeaks publicly announced that it was accepting anonymous Bitcoin donations after conventional payment channels had been restricted.

[Source 1](https://www.forbes.com/sites/andygreenberg/2011/06/14/wikileaks-asks-for-anonymous-bitcoin-donations/) · [Source 2](https://www.wired.com/2011/11/mf-bitcoin/)

**034. 2011-06-19 — First major Mt. Gox security crisis**  
*Supporting event* — A compromised Mt. Gox account was used to dump bitcoin, sending the quoted exchange price from about $17 to pennies before trading was suspended and transactions rolled back.

[Source 1](https://www.wired.com/2011/06/gox/) · [Source 2](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/the-june-2011-bitcoin-flash-crash)

**035. 2011-09-01 — Casascius physical bitcoins appear**  
*Card moment 013* — Mike Caldwell began producing Casascius physical bitcoins: funded Bitcoin private keys embedded beneath tamper-evident holograms on physical coins.

[Source 1](https://bitcoinannotated.com/entries/casascius-coins/)

**036. 2011-10-13 — Litecoin launches**  
*Card moment 014* — Charlie Lee announced Litecoin on 9 October 2011; Litecoin's genesis block was mined about three days later, and the planned public launch time was 13 October.

[Source 1](https://litecoin.com/learning-center/how-was-litecoin-created) · [Source 2](https://litecoin.com/news/deep-dive-to-celebrate-litecoins-12th-birthday)

**037. 2011-11 — Electrum establishes lightweight Bitcoin wallet model**  
*Supporting event* — Electrum was created by Thomas Voegtlin in November 2011 as a lightweight Bitcoin client that could use remote servers instead of downloading the full blockchain.

[Source 1](https://www.electrum.org/)
