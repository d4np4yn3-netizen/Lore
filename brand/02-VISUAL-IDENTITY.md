# LORE — Visual Identity

## Locked logo direction

The approved direction is the **brush-lettered LORE wordmark with a simple crown mark above it**.

The logo should feel hand-drawn, energetic and instantly recognisable rather than corporate or polished to the point of losing personality.

### Primary lockup

Crown mark + LORE brush wordmark + `COLLECT THE INTERNET.`

### Compact lockup

Crown mark + LORE wordmark.

### Micro marks

- Crown mark alone
- Custom L mark for small spaces where the full wordmark becomes unreadable

## Logo rules

- The LORE wordmark must eventually be custom-drawn and owned as a unique silhouette.
- Do not use a generic brush font as the final production logo.
- The crown should be simple enough to work at favicon/app-icon size.
- Avoid excessive Japanese text inside the core logo.
- Japanese-inspired visual language may appear elsewhere in art direction and campaigns, but LORE is a global brand.
- Do not permanently colour-code the master logo by rarity.

## Core colour direction

Primary system:

- **Obsidian** — near-black foundation
- **Bone** — warm off-white
- **Gold** — premium accent

Secondary accents may include deep crimson, midnight blue and rarity-specific colours when relevant.

The brand itself should remain predominantly monochrome/gold so creator artwork and rarity finishes provide the colour.

## Art direction

LORE artwork should draw from the visual energy of premium modern shōnen manga/anime without directly copying any existing series or living artist.

Key qualities:

- confident hand-drawn ink lines
- expressive faces and strong silhouettes
- dynamic perspective
- cinematic lighting
- cel-shaded colour treatment
- bold graphic shapes
- controlled texture and grit
- spectacular supernatural or symbolic visual interpretation where appropriate
- strong storytelling inside a single frame
- premium TCG-level finish

## Anti-drift rules

Do not allow LORE artwork to drift into:

- photorealistic AI portraits with an anime filter
- generic glossy fantasy art
- sexualised default depictions of female creators
- identical “power-up” poses across all creators
- generic crown/throne/fire treatments with no link to the real moment
- direct imitation of One Piece, Demon Slayer, Jujutsu Kaisen, Solo Leveling or any other specific franchise

The creator should remain recognisable, but the card must feel like a true illustrated character interpretation rather than a traced photograph.

## Creator individuality

Each creator needs a distinctive visual language derived from their real history, community, recurring motifs, interests and chosen moments.

Examples:

- a gaming creator might use raid, dungeon, guild or screen motifs
- a finance creator might use charts, currencies, markets or city imagery
- a challenge creator might use arenas, crowds, prizes or spectacle

These motifs must come from creator research, not stereotypes alone.
