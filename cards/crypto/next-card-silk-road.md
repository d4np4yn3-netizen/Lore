# Next card research — Silk Road Shutdown (2013)

Researched 20 September 2026. Dan selected this subject after approving Behind the Chair Common. This document records research and a proposed concept, not visual approval or a final rarity decision.

## Chronology and Bitcoin amounts

| Date | Documented event | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 October 2013 | Ulbricht was arrested in San Francisco and his laptop was seized. | Use this date only for a scene about the arrest. |
| 2 October 2013 | The shutdown was publicly reported; the preserved seizure notice was uploaded that day. | Recommended 02 OCT 2013 for the public website-takedown scene. This is not a claim about an exact server-switch-off timestamp. |
| 25 October 2013 | DOJ announced the seizure of approximately 144,336 BTC recovered from Ulbricht's computer hardware, in addition to approximately 29,655 BTC seized previously. | The user's rounded 144,000 BTC figure belongs to this later seizure announcement, not the initial public takedown day. |

Primary sources:
- [DOJ, 25 October 2013 seizure announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/manhattan-us-attorney-announces-seizure-additional-28-million-worth-bitcoins-belonging). The release describes the extra tranche as worth about $28 million then; don't substitute today's BTC value.
- [FBI, Ulbricht's laptop](https://www.fbi.gov/history/artifacts/ross-william-ulbrichts-laptop): the actual hardware is a useful factual prop reference, separate from drawing style.
- [DOJ, 4 February 2014 indictment announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/manhattan-us-attorney-announces-indictment-ross-ulbricht-creator-and-owner-silk-road): independently distinguishes server funds from Ulbricht's hardware funds.
- [Contemporaneous 2 October 2013 reporting](https://www.wired.com/2013/10/bitcoin-market-drops-600-million-on-silk-road-bust/): records the shutdown becoming public and an immediate price fall followed by recovery.
- [Preserved FBI seizure notice](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Silk_Road_Seized.jpg): factual reference for a website notice, not a drawing-style reference. Its archive date supports public visibility on 2 October.

## Editorial position

A defensible central story is that a hidden online marketplace and its digital wealth were subject to real-world enforcement and seizure. Saying this was the first time the mainstream world recognised Bitcoin's monetary value is too sweeping to establish from these sources. Do not make that assertion as a historical fact.

The event is the 2013 shutdown. Later court outcomes, seizures and political developments should not be mixed into its visual moment. Bitcoin itself did not shut down with the marketplace. Physical piles of coins, gates, bazaar stalls and giant notice banners would be symbolic objects, not literal evidence.

## Proposed direction — The Dark Bazaar

- Card title: SILK / ROAD.
- Subject: BITCOIN.
- Date: 02 OCT 2013, explicitly the public takedown moment.
- Rarity proposal: Legendary, for this defining early crypto-history event. This is an editorial recommendation, not an approved change or statement of moral approval.
- Proposed short caption: SEIZED. A brief notice-style phrase; final wording remains for Dan's selection.
- Main scene: a vast, shadowy digital bazaar rendered as an atmospheric underground market. A huge glowing seizure notice blocks its entrance; the stalls' small lights go out into the distance. In the foreground, an ordinary user recoils from a laptop whose screen shows the takedown message. Restrained cold green/teal screen light, amber lanterns and hard white light from the notice.
- Keep the user and laptop large enough to tell the story at card size. The market is the symbolic depth layer. Avoid repeating the Mt. Gox locked storefront or the failed halving tower's exposed steelwork.

## Three proposed discoveries

1. A small onion-shaped lantern or discreet onion motif: a visual reference to Tor, through which the market was accessed. An invented prop grounded in documented technology.
2. DPR on a faded administrative tag: Ulbricht's Dread Pirate Roberts alias. The placement is fictional; the alias is documented.
3. A small San Francisco evidence docket dated 01 OCT 2013: a reference to the previous day's arrest, distinguished from the card's public-takedown date. This is illustrative, not a reproduction of an actual docket.

Do not put 144,336 BTC on a 2 October in-scene evidence label as though that figure was announced that day. If Dan prefers that number to be the central reveal, the alternate subject is The Seizure, dated 25 OCT 2013, with an investigator and seized computer hardware instead.
