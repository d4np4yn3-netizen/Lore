## The moment

Mt. Gox went offline on 25 February 2014 after halting Bitcoin withdrawals earlier in the month and filed for bankruptcy protection in Tokyo on 28 February.

**Why it earns a card:** The emblematic early exchange collapse; existing approved card.

**Date qualification:** Narrowed from the source collapse range to the bankruptcy date represented on the approved card.

**Approved interpretation to preserve:** Fictional customer outside an imagined Tokyo exchange. Chained glass doors and PENDING display symbolise lost access. Newspaper gives the INITIAL reported 850,000 BTC missing; approximately 200,000 BTC were later located. The address comes from the announcement. The date marks the civil rehabilitation filing, not a one-day theft.

**Approved date / rarity:** 2014-02-28 / epic.

**Locked artwork:** [cards/crypto/season-01/mt-gox-master-01/art.png](https://github.com/d4np4yn3-netizen/Lore/blob/main/cards/crypto/season-01/mt-gox-master-01/art.png).

## Illustration brief

**Scene:** Preserve the approved Locked Out scene: a fictional customer outside an imagined Tokyo exchange, chained glass doors and a pending withdrawal. The emotional centre is helpless waiting as an institution fails.

**Composition:** Retain the approved rain, reflections and locked doors; new clues require explicit revision approval.

**Mood:** Preserve the existing rainy palette.

**Factual boundaries:** Do not depict a single-day theft on the bankruptcy date, assert a real building likeness, or treat initial missing and later recovered amounts as interchangeable.
