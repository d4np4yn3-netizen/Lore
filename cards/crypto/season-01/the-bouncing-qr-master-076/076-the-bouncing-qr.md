# 076 - The Bouncing QR

13 FEB 2022 / COINBASE / COMMON

SUPER BOWL LVI

## The story

On 13 February 2022, the Los Angeles Rams faced the Cincinnati Bengals in Super Bowl LVI. During the television broadcast, Coinbase made its Super Bowl advertising debut with a 60-second spot built around a bouncing QR code. A commercial break became an invitation to investigate an unfamiliar square.

The original code drifted across a black screen, changing colour as it met the edges, recalling a DVD screensaver. Instead of explaining cryptocurrency through a celebrity or elaborate story, the advert asked viewers to act. Scanning connected the television image to a Coinbase promotional page on their phones.

Coinbase reported more than 20 million landing-page hits in one minute. That figure described traffic, not 20 million new customers or unique viewers. The company said demand exceeded its projections and temporarily throttled its systems. The rush demonstrated the response to a small graphic on a large stage.

The artwork imagines that interruption inside an American sports bar. Fans in Rams blue and gold and Bengals black and orange face the same television. Their shirts suggest allegiance without recreating the teams' exact game uniforms. A football in a display case anchors the gathering in sports culture.

The nearly empty screen becomes the room's centre of attention. This fictional audience represents a wider shift: crypto companies were reaching people far beyond specialist communities. THE BOUNCING QR remembers how a simple visual prompt turned an expensive broadcast moment into something viewers could choose to explore themselves.

## Details in the artwork

1. **THE DIGITAL QR** The television code recalls the advert's invitation to scan. This artwork uses HISTROVE's public digital destination, /scan/076/, rather than Coinbase's original signup page. It is an interpretive replacement, not a reproduction of the historical code.
2. **THE BLACK SCREEN** The broad black space around the code recalls the original commercial's stripped-back presentation. Its emptiness interrupts the warm, detailed room. The still image suggests the moving advert without inventing a visible trail or additional message.
3. **THE OPPOSING COLOURS** Blue and gold on the left recall Rams supporters; black and orange on the right recall Bengals supporters. The fictional fans share one screen. These emblem-free shirts suggest team loyalties, not exact Super Bowl uniforms.
4. **THE FOOTBALL CASE** A football rests in a clear display case beneath the television. Together with the framed sports pictures, it establishes the fictional bar's atmosphere. It is an invented prop, with no claimed signature or match provenance.
