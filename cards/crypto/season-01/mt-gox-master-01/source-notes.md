# Mt. Gox: research and creative interpretation

## Event and date
28 FEB 2014 marks MtGox Co., Ltd.'s application for commencement of civil rehabilitation at the Tokyo District Court, accepted that day. It is not an assertion that the theft happened in a single incident that day. The company said the website closed at noon Japan time on 25 February.
Primary company announcement: https://www.mtgox.com/img/pdf/20140228-announcement_eng.pdf (pp. 1–3).

## Three clues
- Newspaper: “850,000 BTC / REPORTED / MISSING”. The 28 February announcement reported approximately 750,000 customer BTC and 100,000 company BTC missing. The paper design is invented and this is a paraphrased initial report, not a photographed newspaper or final loss figure.
- Address plaque: “2-11-5”, from the company's listed Shibuya 2-11-5, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo address. The surrounding building is imagined, not an architectural reconstruction.
- “WITHDRAWAL / PENDING”: fictional screen representing customers' inability to access their holdings. Not an assertion that Mt. Gox had such a physical terminal. Receipt is likewise fictional.

The 20 March company announcement states an old-format wallet was found on 7 March to contain approximately 200,000 BTC, revising estimated missing BTC to about 650,000. The card depicts the initial February announcement; it does not claim that 850,000 BTC were permanently lost.
Source: https://www.mtgox.com/img/pdf/20140320-btc-announce.pdf (English, p. 2).
