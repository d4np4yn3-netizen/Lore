# LORE — Moment Research Standard

## Core principle

**Six Moments. One Icon.**

Every creator collection must be grounded in research into that creator's genuine public history before artwork begins.

LORE cards should preserve and reinterpret real moments, not invent six generic transformations.

## Research target

For each creator:

1. Gather at least **20 candidate moments**.
2. Source each candidate with the original or best-available public reference.
3. Score the candidates.
4. Shortlist the strongest **10**.
5. Present the shortlist to the creator/partner.
6. Agree the final **6**.
7. Only then commission or generate production artwork.

## Valid source types

Candidate moments can come from:

- X posts / tweets
- YouTube videos
- livestream clips
- podcasts
- interviews
- launch moments
- records and milestones
- memes strongly associated with the creator
- community moments
- charity/community activity
- public achievements
- public failures or funny moments the creator is comfortable celebrating
- creator-selected personal favourites

## Selection criteria

Score each candidate against:

### Recognition
Will the creator's audience immediately understand the reference?

### Story
Does the moment say something meaningful about the creator's journey?

### Visual potential
Can the moment become exceptional illustrated card art?

### Shareability
Would the creator or community be likely to repost the card?

### Source quality
Is there a stable, verifiable original source?

### Longevity
Will the moment still make sense years later?

### Creator comfort
Is the creator likely to be proud or amused to have it permanently represented?

## Required balance

A six-card collection should not consist of six versions of the same type of event.

Aim for a balanced story such as:

- origin / early moment
- breakout moment
- community or meme moment
- major achievement
- defining cultural moment
- creator's own choice

This is guidance, not a mandatory chronology.

## Rarity assignment

Do not assume:

- earliest = Common
- newest = Mythic
- most commercially successful = Mythic

Rarity should consider cultural significance, visual potential, collector appeal and product storytelling.

The Common can be a beloved moment. The Mythic should usually be a defining or visually extraordinary moment, but the creator's final six should work as a complete story rather than a simple ladder of fame.

## Artwork interpretation

The artwork should **interpret** the moment, not simply reproduce a screenshot.

Example:

A creator's huge challenge video might become a vast anime arena with the creator commanding the spectacle.

A famous post could become a symbolic scene based on the idea and community reaction surrounding the post.

The factual moment remains the anchor; the visual treatment becomes legendary.

## Creator approval

Before a moment becomes final artwork, record:

- creator approval status
- source URL
- short factual description
- working card title
- rarity
- visual concept
- sensitive/context notes if needed

Creators should be able to reject or replace moments they do not want immortalised.

## No invented lore presented as fact

LORE may use fantasy visual language, but the underlying story copy must distinguish real history from artistic interpretation.

Never invent achievements, quotations, incidents or creator history.
