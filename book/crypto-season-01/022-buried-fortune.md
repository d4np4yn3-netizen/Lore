# 022 - Buried Fortune

**Crypto · Season One · Rare · 2013**

**Approved phrase:** ONE DRIVE. A FORTUNE. (editorial caption, not a quotation)

## The story

In 2009, James Howells mined bitcoin on a laptop in Newport, Wales. What later made his old hard drive extraordinary was the wallet information it held: a record of the private key needed to spend his bitcoin. The fortune existed on a distributed network, but access depended on information stored in a small physical object.

During a clear-out in 2013, the drive was mistakenly discarded and taken to Newport's Docksway landfill. By November, Howells had realised what was missing. Contemporary reports put the inaccessible holding's value above £4 million. Landfill staff told him the drive could already lie beneath several feet of rubbish, somewhere within an area the size of a football pitch. The broad destination was known; the drive's exact position was not.

For years, Howells sought permission to search the landfill and assembled expertise and financial backing for a recovery attempt. The council opposed excavation, citing environmental and safety concerns. Finding the hardware was only part of the challenge: its information would also need to remain readable. The story exposed a practical limit of digital ownership. A record could persist on the network while its owner lacked the key needed to use it.

On 9 January 2025, the High Court dismissed Howells's claim against Newport City Council. It held that the discarded drive belonged to the council under waste-disposal law; permission to appeal was subsequently refused. The ruling concerned the hardware, not ownership of his bitcoin. BURIED FORTUNE looks back to the 2013 loss, when an ordinary piece of discarded equipment became the centre of an extraordinary problem.

## Details in the artwork

1. **The 2009 label.** The handwritten tape points to 2009, when Howells mined the bitcoin. It is an invented label, not an authenticated mark on the lost drive. The disposal happened in 2013.
2. **Docks Way / Newport.** The fence sign locates the scene at Newport's Docksway landfill. Its wording and weathered design are fictional props, rather than a reproduction of a verified historical sign.
3. **The WAVES cup.** The partly buried coffee cup returns from 017, CASH OUT, and 018, FOUR FIGURES. It is a visual collection callback; no historical connection between this cup and the lost drive is claimed.
4. **The buried drive.** The oversized drive and exposed rubbish layers make an unseen loss visible. Their scale and position are symbolic. The drive held a private-key record; the bitcoin themselves were never physically buried.
