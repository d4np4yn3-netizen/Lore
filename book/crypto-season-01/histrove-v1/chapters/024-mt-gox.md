# 024 - Mt. Gox

**Crypto · Season One · Epic · 28 February 2014**

**Approved phrase:** WITHDRAWALS SUSPENDED. (editorial caption, not a quotation)

## The story

Rain runs down the glass, but the message behind it does not change. A customer stands outside with a piece of paper in hand. The screen says the withdrawal is pending; the chains say something worse. This is HISTROVE’s imagined doorway into the collapse of Mt. Gox, where an ordinary action, taking out your own money, had become a question nobody could answer.

On 28 February 2014, the Tokyo-based exchange applied to the Tokyo District Court for civil rehabilitation, a form of insolvency protection. Its website had closed three days earlier. In its announcement, the company said approximately 750,000 customer bitcoins and 100,000 of its own had disappeared. These were preliminary reported figures, issued while the causes and extent of the losses were still being investigated.

The number on the newspaper therefore belongs to that first account of the crisis. On 20 March, Mt. Gox announced that it had located approximately 200,000 BTC in an old-format wallet, revising its estimate of missing bitcoin to about 650,000. The discovery mattered, but it did not turn the locked door into an open one. Customers had entered a legal process whose questions would take far longer to resolve.

The scene holds on that gap between a number on a screen and the ability to use it. Bitcoin could keep moving across its network while an exchange failed to return the funds in its care. For the figure in the rain, the distinction offers little immediate comfort. HISTROVE places the promise of easy transfer beside a padlock, making custody and trust part of the collection’s story.

## Details in the artwork

1. **The first reported total.** The newspaper’s 850,000 BTC refers to the initial missing amount. About 200,000 BTC were later located. The paper is fictional; its headline preserves the first report’s uncertainty.
2. **The locked door.** Chains, a padlock and the withdrawal-pending display turn lost access into a visible barrier. This is an imagined exchange entrance, not a likeness of a documented Mt. Gox building.
3. **A paper plane stopped short.** The white plane beside the chains recalls 007, The First Transfer. Its placement sets the collection’s image of money moving freely against this scene of waiting and obstruction.
4. **An envelope from the future.** The envelope marked 2024 looks ahead to Bitcoin and Bitcoin Cash repayments that July. It is an intentional, anachronistic collection clue, not mail present at the 2014 filing.

## Source and art note

Mt. Gox’s [28 February announcement](https://www.mtgox.com/img/pdf/20140228-announcement_eng.pdf) records the civil-rehabilitation application and initial missing amounts; its [20 March update](https://www.mtgox.com/img/pdf/20140320-btc-announce.pdf) reports the old-wallet discovery. A [5 July 2024 trustee notice](https://www.mtgox.com/img/pdf/20240705_01_announcement_en.pdf) confirms Bitcoin and Bitcoin Cash repayments to some creditors. The card’s date marks a filing, not a one-day theft. The customer, rainy entrance and newspaper are fictional. WITHDRAWALS SUSPENDED is editorial copy; the 2024 envelope deliberately looks forward.
