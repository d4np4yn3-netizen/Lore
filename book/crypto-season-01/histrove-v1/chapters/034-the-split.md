# 034 - The Split

**Crypto · Season One · Epic · 20 July 2016**

**Phrase:** ONE HISTORY. TWO CHAINS. Editorial caption; not a historical quotation.

## The story

At sunset, one railway line opens into two routes. Two travellers stand beside the switch with maps in their hands. One map carries a return arrow; the other a padlock. The track behind them is shared. Ahead, the signs point to ETH and ETC. This imagined junction gives a physical shape to the disagreement that divided Ethereum in the summer of 2016.

The 17 June DAO attack had left the community facing a decision about an application built on Ethereum. Should the network intervene to help recover the funds, or preserve the contract's outcome? On 20 July 2016, at block 1,920,000, the hard fork enacted a specific change to the chain's state. Balances from DAO-related contracts moved into a recovery contract, allowing DAO token holders to reclaim Ether.

The chain that adopted the intervention continued as Ethereum, using ETH. Participants who rejected the change kept the original chain running; it became Ethereum Classic, using ETC. Their objection centred on the principle that an application's failure should not justify changing the underlying chain's rules. The fork's supporters accepted an exceptional intervention to return funds. A common history now had two continuing networks.

The return arrow and padlock simplify that argument into symbols. Neither traveller is a named historical participant, and neither route is a judgement on which future was right. The fork did not rewrite earlier transaction records; it changed particular balances at a defined block. Beyond that point, the two chains could record different events. The railway captures the lasting consequence of a community that could no longer agree on one path.

## Details in the artwork

1. **The two maps.** The return arrow represents recovery; the padlock represents the case for preserving the original rules. They symbolise opposing positions without identifying real people.
2. **The shared sleepers.** The incoming track and wooden sleepers evoke a common history before the divergence. Both routes begin from the same railway.
3. **Block 1,920,000.** The numbered marker identifies the block at which the DAO recovery fork took effect on 20 July 2016. It is a block height, not an amount of Ether.
4. **The refund station.** The station and sealed envelopes turn the recovery contract into a familiar scene of funds being returned. The office and envelopes are invented symbols of an on-chain process.
5. **ETH and ETC.** The two signs identify Ethereum and Ethereum Classic. ETH marks the chain that adopted the recovery change; ETC marks the continuation without it.
