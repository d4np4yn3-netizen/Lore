# 017 - Cash Out

**Crypto · Season One · Common · 29 October 2013**

**Approved phrase:** TAP BUY OR SELL. (continuous excerpt from Jordan Kelley's launch interview with CoinDesk; uppercase and punctuation normalized)

## The story

A customer reaches for a banknote while the coffee machine hums behind her. Outside, the illustrated city is wet and blue; inside, a bright screen lights her face. The surprise is small and immediate: something held in a digital wallet has become cash she can take away. No vault or trading floor is needed, just a new machine in an ordinary coffee shop.

On 29 October 2013, a Robocoin kiosk launched at Waves Coffee House in downtown Vancouver. Operated by Bitcoiniacs, it marked the first permanent public Bitcoin ATM installation. Earlier demonstrations had shown the idea at conferences; this machine put it somewhere people could walk in, order a drink and try it for themselves.

The kiosk worked in both directions. Customers could exchange cash for bitcoin or sell bitcoin and collect cash. A phone reader and a separate palm scanner formed part of its unusual hardware. In a launch interview, Robocoin chief executive Jordan Kelley described the simple choice: tap buy or sell. The woman here demonstrates the cash-out side; she is a fictional customer, not a portrait of the first person to use it.

This moment gave an online currency a familiar physical doorway. The machine did not create bitcoins or turn them into metal coins. It connected a digital balance with a cash transaction, while the cafe around it carried on serving coffee. That ordinary setting is the point of the picture: a new way to exchange money appearing between the counter, the cups and the next person in line.

## Details in the artwork

1. **THE BLUE ROBOCOIN.** The blue-and-silver cabinet and black screen surround follow contemporary launch photographs. Its readable interface is simplified for the illustration, not a recreation of a documented withdrawal screen.
2. **TWO SEPARATE READERS.** The red-lit phone reader and green-lit palm scanner are distinct from the cash outlet. They recall the original kiosk's hardware; the cash comes from the slot below the main screen.
3. **THE WAVES CUP.** The takeaway cup names the coffee-house setting. It is this scene's everyday anchor and a possible recurring object for future HISTROVE artwork, not a claim that this exact cup was photographed at the launch.
4. **THE LITTLE KEY.** A reporter found the ATM's key on a cafe table during installation the day before launch. The small brass key beside the cup is a callback to that incident; its placement here is invented.
5. **CASH IN HAND.** The green Canadian-style note makes the exchange visible. The customer, denomination, evening rain and city view are illustrative choices, not a documented first withdrawal or exact reconstruction of the launch.

## Source and art note

[CoinDesk's launch interview](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2013/10/30/robocoin-launches-bitcoin-atm-in-vancouver) records the operator, buy/sell function and Kelley's words. [WIRED's advance report](https://www.wired.com/2013/10/worlds-first-bitcoin-atm/) distinguishes the permanent installation from earlier demonstrations; its [launch photographs](https://www.wired.com/gallery/bitcoinatmgallery/) document the hardware. The [28 October installation report](https://www.wired.com/2013/10/robocoin-here/) supplies the key anecdote. People, weather, composition, props and screen wording are dramatized. Earlier test use must not be relabelled as the first cash withdrawal.
