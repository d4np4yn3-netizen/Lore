# 029 - Keys in Your Pocket

**Crypto · Season One · Common · 29 July 2014**

**Phrase:** THE BITCOIN SAFE. (period Trezor product tagline, not a spoken quotation)

## The story

A wallet owner holds a small black device towards us. Behind it, a computer screen flashes red warnings. The room is dim, but a desk lamp keeps the face, hands and wallet in warm light. The scene makes a technical distinction visible: the machine connected to the internet does not have to be the place that holds the keys used to authorize a bitcoin transaction.

On 29 July 2014, the original Trezor, now known as the Model One, officially launched. Its founders, Marek "Slush" Palatinus and Pavol "Stick" Rusnak, had developed a single-purpose computer designed to isolate private keys from the everyday host machine. A small physical object gave self-custody a different form: something its owner could connect, inspect and operate separately from the computer's software.

The device's own screen and two buttons matter to that separation. They allow the owner to check transaction details and confirm an action on the hardware. The drawing condenses this into a simple "Confirm?" prompt. It represents the function rather than reproducing an exact launch-day interface. Bitcoin itself remains recorded on the blockchain; the device protects the signing keys used to authorize spending. The warning-filled monitor is an imagined threat, not a record of a particular hack.

The recovery sheet brings the responsibility back to the owner. Keeping keys apart from a computer is one part of custody; preserving a usable backup is another. Its empty lines suggest that preparation without displaying secret words. THE BITCOIN SAFE was early Trezor product wording, visible on pre-2016 packaging. Here it names the device's purpose without making a promise of invulnerability. The pocket-sized wallet offers a separate point of control, while care and judgment still belong to the person holding it.

## Details in the artwork

1. **Separate confirmation.** The device's screen and two physical buttons reference independent review and confirmation. The simplified "Confirm?" display is illustrative, not a verified July 2014 firmware screen.
2. **The recovery sheet.** Blank numbered lines recall the need for an offline wallet backup. No secret words are shown, and the visible line count is not presented as a verified 2014 standard. The two loose sheets remain deliberately blank.
3. **Two sources of light.** The warm upper-left lamp and red computer glow make the contrast between owner control and a threatening host visible. The person, room and warning screen are an invented scene, not a documented launch photograph.

## Source and art note

Trezor's [ten-year history](https://trezor.io/blog/news/a-decade-of-pioneering-10-years-since-trezors-first-hardware-wallet-revolution) dates the launch and describes its founders' approach. Its [Model One authentication guide](https://trezor.io/guides/trezor-devices/trezor-model-one/authenticate-model-one) shows the old THE BITCOIN SAFE packaging; its [hardware security guide](https://trezor.io/learn/security-privacy/how-trezor-keeps-you-safe/trezor-hardware-built-in-security) explains the screen and buttons. Current guides establish functions, not every detail of 2014 firmware or packaging.
