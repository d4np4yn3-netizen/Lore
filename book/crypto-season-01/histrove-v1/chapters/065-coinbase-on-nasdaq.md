# 065 - Coinbase on Nasdaq

14 APR 2021 / PUBLIC MARKETS / UNCOMMON

IT’S ONLY THE BEGINNING.

## The story

On 14 April 2021, Coinbase completed the direct listing of its Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker COIN. A company built around access to crypto now had shares trading on a major public stock exchange. The listed security was Coinbase stock, not bitcoin.

That morning, Coinbase marked the occasion with a remote Nasdaq Opening Bell ceremony. Participants joined from across the country, and the digital countdown lasted 21 seconds. Nasdaq explicitly linked the number to the maximum 21 million bitcoin that can be mined, bringing a familiar crypto reference into the exchange's ritual.

The direct-listing process matters to the story. Nasdaq's trader alert explained that COIN would remain in a regulatory halt until the exchange opened trading. Buy and sell orders in the opening auction would determine the opening public price. The bell celebration and the stock's first trade were separate events.

## Details in the artwork

1. **THE TWENTY-ONE COUNTDOWN** The giant 21 recalls the ceremony's 21-second digital countdown. Nasdaq identified it as a reference to Bitcoin's maximum supply of 21 million. It is a countdown clue, not a stock price.
2. **THE CLASS A SHARES** COIN / CLASS A identifies Coinbase's ticker and the class of common stock listed on the Nasdaq Global Select Market. The strip distinguishes shares in the company from the crypto assets on its platform.
3. **THE DIRECT LISTING** DIRECT LISTING names the route Coinbase used to go public. Nasdaq's opening auction was to establish the first public trading price. This label is an explanatory addition to the imagined display.
4. **THE REMOTE CELEBRATION** Four video windows recall a ceremony conducted remotely across the country. These illustrated participants are fictional; neither their identities nor the number and arrangement of windows reproduce an exact broadcast frame.
