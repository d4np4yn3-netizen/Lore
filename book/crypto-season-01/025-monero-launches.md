# 025 - Monero Launches

**Crypto · Season One · Rare · 18 April 2014**

**Approved phrase:** PRIVACY FOR ALL. (editorial caption, not a quotation)

## The story

A warm lamp hangs above the stall. A customer and a seller face each other across fruit, fabric and everyday clutter, while the market carries on behind them. LORE puts privacy in the middle of ordinary life: a payment between two people who do not need to share their whole financial history with everyone passing by.

The Monero blockchain went live on 18 April 2014, initially under the name BitMonero. Its launch had been announced in advance on BitcoinTalk, and the project began without a premine or token sale. Built on CryptoNote technology, it took a different approach to transaction privacy from Bitcoin’s openly traceable ledger. The shorter name, Monero, followed later that month as the community began shaping the project.

CryptoNote’s design combined one-time destination keys with ring signatures, aiming to make payments harder to link and their source harder to identify. Those foundations should not be confused with every protection Monero later acquired. Minimum ring-size requirements and Ring Confidential Transactions arrived through subsequent upgrades; RingCT, which conceals transaction amounts, was enabled in 2017. The launch began a continuing effort, rather than completing a promise of perfect anonymity.

The market gives that ambition a human scale. Privacy here is room to conduct an ordinary transaction without turning a person into a trail for spectators to follow. Three plain tokens suggest the related goal of fungibility: units of money that can be treated interchangeably. The awnings divide the crowded scene into small pockets of personal space, while the seller’s lamp keeps the exchange itself clear and approachable.

## Details in the artwork

1. **The Blind Signatures envelope.** The cream envelope marked Blind Signatures and 1982 returns from 001. It is a collection link to an earlier privacy idea, not evidence that Monero uses Chaum’s original system.
2. **CryptoNote on the shelf.** The book names the technology behind Monero’s beginnings. Its binding and market placement are fictional, but the connection to CryptoNote is historical and central to the launch.
3. **A spiral in the awning.** The small spiral foreshadows the later Tornado Cash privacy debate. This intentionally forward-looking motif makes a thematic connection; it does not claim shared founders, code or organisational ties.
4. **Three plain tokens.** The shallow tray holds three interchangeable-looking tokens, a visual metaphor for fungibility. They are not physical Monero coins or a reconstruction of how a launch-day payment occurred.
